# Story architecture

## Episode contract

Define the episode before drafting:

- **Audience promise**: the specific dramatic experience or answer delivered in this episode.
- **Want**: what the protagonist consciously tries to obtain now.
- **Need**: the internal truth or capability the protagonist lacks; it may remain partly unresolved across episodes.
- **Opposition**: an active person, system, rule, environment, belief, or cost that pushes back.
- **Stakes**: what worsens if the protagonist does nothing or chooses badly.
- **Opening and ending states**: observable differences in information, emotion, power, risk, relationship, or physical conditions.
- **Episode payoff**: a real result for the promise, even when a larger season question remains open.

## Opening Hook

The Hook is the opening causal unit, not an isolated teaser. Bind it to the first Beat. Choose one primary mechanism from `../../short-video-production/references/opening-hook-and-five-second-retention.md`, then define the first visible/audible event, the observable state change in roughly the first five seconds, the question still unresolved at that moment, and the later Beat that pays off the opening promise.

The Hook must be truthful to the episode. It may withhold context, but it may not substitute an unrelated spectacular image, invented danger, or a claim the story never delivers. A quiet hold, reaction, sound-image contradiction, or slow reveal is valid when it creates a precise knowledge or danger gap; rapid cutting is not required.

## Causal Beat design

Use a Beat when one consequential dramatic unit completes. Join Beats with causality:

```text
B001 causes or forces B002
B002 meets resistance, therefore the character chooses B003
B003 changes the situation, but creates B004
```

Reject Beats that only decorate tone, repeat known information, or move a character without changing the dramatic state. Merge or remove them unless they provide necessary setup whose payoff is recorded.

Every Beat must change at least one closed domain:

- `information`
- `emotion`
- `power`
- `risk`
- `relationship`
- `physical`

Record the transition as `domain: before -> after`. A Beat may change several domains, but one must be primary.

## Choice and escalation

- Give the protagonist a choice under pressure whenever the plot meaningfully turns.
- Escalate cost, constraint, uncertainty, intimacy, exposure, or irreversible consequence; do not merely make actions louder.
- Let coincidence create a problem, clue, or encounter. Do not let coincidence deliver the central solution.
- Make the climax resolve the episode's central choice through established character capability, cost, relationship, rule, or setup.

## Information and reveals

Track audience knowledge separately from each character's knowledge. A reveal needs:

1. a source the story has established;
2. a Beat where the information becomes available;
3. a receiver who can reasonably notice or learn it;
4. a consequence that changes later behavior;
5. protection against accidental early disclosure.

For mystery or horror, delay explanation without withholding every meaningful clue. Suspense comes from the gap between knowledge and danger, not from arbitrary confusion.

## Setup and payoff

Record every plot-critical object, rule, ability, relationship promise, repeated image, or suspicious fact that later controls a solution or reveal. A payoff must be prepared early enough to feel earned and specific enough to be recognized.

If an apparent setup intentionally remains open for a later episode, record its target Episode or season function rather than calling it paid off.

## Episodic ending

End the episode after delivering its current promise. The continuing hook should arise from:

- a consequence of the protagonist's choice;
- a newly visible cost;
- a changed relationship or power balance;
- an earned reveal that redefines the next objective;
- an unfinished larger goal with a concrete new obstacle.

Do not cut randomly before the current dramatic unit pays off.
